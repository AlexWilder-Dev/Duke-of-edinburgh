## The building and the garden

- **Outside.** Two storeys plus an attic under a steep hipped roof of clay tiles. Thin red bricks in English bond, with brick mullion-and-transom windows. The original metal casements keep their square leaded lights and stained glass. Three big chimneystacks. The splayed corner is a Truman's inter-war trademark. Carved stone panels over the doors carry the Truman's eagle, above wrought-iron fanlights. A datestone reads 1936. Rolling-in doors on Bythorn Street let draymen roll barrels straight into the cellar. Today the fascia is two white boards, THE DUKE OF and EDINBURGH, either side of the stone relief. A painted ghost sign on the side wall reads "If it's TRUMAN'S it is BEER!". The 2025 exterior photo has a dog in it.
- **Inside.** Light oak panelling. CAMRA notes counters with doors for servicing the old beer engines, and the Cole report (Historic England, 2015) says the panelling is inlaid with the names of the brewery's 1930s beers ("EAGLE STOUT" is visible above the bar back). Embossed Truman's mirrors over the fireplaces. Three-part oak bar backs, each under "a trio of shallow four-centred arches". A chequer-pattern tiled border runs along the saloon counter (CAMRA: the "chequerwork spittoon trough"). There is a stone inglenook with a Tudor-arched fireplace in the old lounge and billiard room. The publican's office inside the servery is a "very rare survival", and so is the off sales compartment. The rooms have been opened up: "you can now perambulate through the whole pub" (CAMRA). The red baize pool table sits in front of cream subway tiles and maroon walls. Big globe pendants hang over round oak tables. The bar sign reads THE DUKE *of Edinburgh* · GINS · WHISKYS · RUMS, in vermilion serif caps with a gold outline. Plants sit on every sill.
- **Light.** Low autumn sun comes through the leaded windows onto the oak. The June 2026 film photos are warm and grainy: golden umbrellas, festoon bulbs, hanging baskets.
- **Garden.** The pub's own claim is "South London's biggest & best beer garden". Mustard umbrellas over picnic benches with painted numbers. Timber pergolas with greenery and sunflowers, and festoon lights. The jumbo screen sits under a timber canopy at the far end, with patio heaters. The listing says the crazy paving and low stone walls may be from the 1930s scheme, and CAMRA notes the garden runs alongside the railway embankment. In 1964 British Movietone filmed roll curling here, on what Brixton Buzz (2015) calls "the first ever concrete rink in the UK".
- **The disco ball.** It hangs over the dance floor inside. It appears in a 2025 close-up and in the June 2026 crowd shot, and the 16 Oct 2026 post says "The disco ball is spinning".
- **History.** A pub stood here by 1871 (licence transfer in the Morning Advertiser, via PubWiki) and is thought to date from c1860, when the road was Shepherd's Lane. It was rebuilt in 1936–37 for Truman, Hanbury, Buxton & Co. by A. E. Sewell. Frank Herbert Bailey held the licence across the rebuild (1919–1944). Victor H. Bailey ran it in 1965. Solitaire Restaurants (now Rose Pubs) took over in August 2013, and it was listed in 2015. In November 2019 Lambeth issued a new licence with about 80 conditions, including a garden cap and silent outside TVs; that is the root of "commentary inside only". CAMRA rates the interior Two Star: "A pub interior of very special national historic interest".

## Who comes when

- **Mon–Thu, 3–7pm.** After-work crowd for happy hour. The kitchen opens at 4. Jerk Valley deals run Mon, Tue and Wed, plus Wine Wednesday. The pub only opens at 3pm on these days, so these are the quiet days.
- **Weeknights.** Champions League, and The Celebrity Traitors on the big screen on Thursdays at 8pm (Oct 2026 posts). Gig-goers come before shows at O2 Academy Brixton, "a 6 min walk": the pub posts the week's Academy line-ups and offers a bag drop at the bar.
- **Friday.** Open till midnight. Friday DJ/Aperol nights ran through summer 2026. "Friday Feels" themed nights were last seen in April 2026.
- **Saturday.** Rugby and football all day, a full garden, DJs, open till 1am.
- **Sunday.** Jerk Valley roasts 12–8pm (while stocks last), Sunday happy hour 12–6, families.
- **Families** come in daylight only: under-18s until 7pm Sun–Thu and 5pm Fri–Sat, earlier on big sporting days.
- **Dogs**: "completely dog friendly", any day.
- **Groups.** Birthdays, engagements, leaving dos and work Christmas parties: DesignMyNight has 682 reviews, mostly from groups. World Cup 2026 games were "fully booked". In July 2026 the pub closed for one England match "out of respect to our neighbours… we need to protect our license".

## What reviewers say

Ratings: Google about 4.2 from 1,583 (via Thatsup, June 2026; Google itself needs JavaScript). DesignMyNight 4/5 from 682. TripAdvisor about 3.5 from 85 (via MapQuest; TripAdvisor returns 403).

Praise:
- The garden comes first, in about 38% of DMN reviews. Time Out ranks it #2 of London's best beer gardens (10 Sep 2026): "This popular Brixton boozer has a whopper of a beer garden. One of the biggest in the area, in fact." Barratt London ranked it #1 in July 2025 for sun: "The Duke of Edinburgh scored exceptionally highly for average daily sun coverage, helping it to clinch our top spot."
- Jerk Valley food, about 33%. Hot Dinners, 5 Mar 2026: "South London's biggest beer garden could easily coast by on the size of its alfresco offering alone, but the food here is as full-on as the space itself."
- Friendly bar and booking staff, the atmosphere, sport on the big screen, and good for big groups.
- Time Out, Brixton pubs (Mar 2026): "A Brixton staple with a huge beer garden, the Duke of Edinburgh ticks all of the boxes of a classic pub."
- The listed Truman's interior (CAMRA).

Complaints:
- Door staff and security attitude. This drives most 1-star reviews.
- Sport: no sound outside, poor sightlines from some booked tables, and booked events not shown.
- Table allocation: tables too small, a World Cup double-booking (July 2026), and disputes over the £25 hold.
- The garden shuts at 9:30pm, and the under-18 cut-off.
- Long bar queues on busy days; tired toilets; uneven food; no cask ale (handpumps out since Jan 2023).
- One recent TripAdvisor review (May 2026) alleges discriminatory door policy.

The site can't fix the door. It can be upfront about the rules people trip over: the hold, the hour, the garden time, the commentary and the cut-off. It shows them before every booking, and in context everywhere else.

## What's weak about the current site

- The same booking-rules block sits above the content on every page, so the actual page starts a screen down on a phone.
- Menus are PDF only, in A4 landscape. The Sunday PDF's prices sit apart from their dishes. There's no V/VE filter and no way to read a menu one-handed at the bar.
- Sport is one long list with team crest images. Every BOOK button goes to DesignMyNight with the date but not the kick-off.
- The "Open Today" banner gives the hours but can't say whether the garden or kitchen is open right now, or what's on.

## Design reasoning

- **Palette.** Plum #392544 is the ink of the circle logo and the theme's `--bg-colour`. Cream #f5efc3 and bottle green #014f41 are the theme's hover pair, and the green is also on their 2024 sport posters. Oak #cd8446 and #86502a come from the saloon bar counter. Brick #8a3b2c is the facade. Mustard #e3a62f is the garden umbrellas. Two borrowed palettes stay on their own pages. The food page uses Jerk Valley's in-pub menu colours (orange-red #f04b23, sand #dcd4c0, black). Christmas uses their own Christmas banner's pink #f7c0c3 and red #a84140.
- **Type.** Bebas Neue and Poppins are the theme's `--font-secondary` and `--font-primary`. Space Mono and Young Serif match the 2026 drinks menu: mono section heads ("✷ SHOOTERS") and a soft serif for the drink names. The serif also suits the bar's painted signboard. The eight-point ✷ is their drinks-menu and poster bullet.
- **Building motifs.** The square leaded lights become the background lattice; their drinks menu sits on the same grid. The bar back's shallow four-centred arches become the photo frames. The saloon counter's chequer tiles become the section break. The facade's two fascia boards become the header ("THE DUKE OF · logo · EDINBURGH"). The splayed corner becomes the chamfered corners on buttons and tags.
- **Signature interactions**, both taken from how the pub actually works. (1) "Today at the Duke" reads the London clock. It says whether you can get in, whether the garden is still open, whether the kitchen is serving, whether happy hour is on, what today's deal is and whether kids can still come. It draws the day as lanes on a ribbon, and you can swipe through the week. `?at=2026-11-08T14:00` sets the clock for demos. (2) The jumbo-screen fixtures board: Book opens the booking sheet with the match's date and kick-off filled in. The disco ball turns up on Saturdays.
- **Photos.** Everything is theirs, from catalogue/wp-content/uploads/: June 2026 film shots (garden, jumbo screen, DJ, disco crowd), 2023 interior and garden shots, Jerk Valley food (Jan 2025) and the Jerk Valley festive shoot (Aug 2025).

## What the owners most want this site to do

1. Fill tables: Christmas parties now, sport and weekend groups all year. Bookings sit on every page of their site, and the Christmas page and fixture list lead.
2. Bring people in Monday to Thursday: Jerk Valley's Mon–Wed deals, happy hour, Wine Wednesday and the Thursday screenings. These are the 3pm-opening days they keep pushing on Instagram.
3. Sell the garden and the screens: "South London's biggest & best beer garden" is the H1, the Instagram bio and the press. The bloody-big screen now stays out all year.
